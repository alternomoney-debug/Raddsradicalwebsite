
The canteen. Or the bar. The dream can't decide, so it's both. Fryer oil and floor cleaner. A game on a screen above the taps that nobody is watching. Long tables, the facility kind, bolted down.

I'm carrying a bag of groceries. I don't know why. I know why. The soup here is bad.

"You're back," Nelson says. Doesn't look up from his book.

"Transit was delayed."

"Heard." A page turns. "Soup's still bad."

"That's why I went to the market."

He looks at the bag. Back to the book. "Mind if I bum a can off ya."

"I don't."

He puts the book down, face-first, spine up. "I'll get bowls, then."

Two bowls. Mismatched. One chipped at the rim. Up, bowls, done. Same as always. That part the dream gets right.

The rest of the table is full.

It wasn't, a second ago. It is now. Fifteen people, maybe. I count. Fourteen. I count again. Sixteen. The number won't hold still.

Numbers always hold still. That's how I know where I am.

---

I get up. The bowl stays. Nelson stays. He's reading again.

I go down the table one seat at a time. No plan. Whoever's next.

---

Sari Wijaya. Software engineer. The one whose seat I took.

She has a laptop open and won't put it down. Same as the demo bay. Same as always. She looks up when I sit across from her and says something in Indonesian, quick, the way she talked when a build was failing and she was the only one who knew why.

The dream translates. Dreams do that.

"You took the cracked one," she says.

"It works."

"Half of it works."

"Half is honest."

She almost laughs. Goes back to her screen. "Look after the ship. It doesn't tell you when it's hurt. You have to read the logs."

"I read the logs."

"All of them?"

"Four hundred pages of eleven thousand."

"Then you haven't read the logs." She types something. Doesn't look up. "Keep going."

She's dead. I know that here. Two hundred and forty-nine, and she was one of them. I was off-site that week, running a check on launch infrastructure. That's the whole reason I'm the one at this table with a pulse.

I don't say any of that. She knows. She's in the dream.

---

Lev Karpov. Pilot. Paws in his pockets. Tired eyes.

"Who's flying?" he says.

"Nobody. We're asleep."

"Someone's always flying." He looks up, at the ceiling of the canteen, like there's something past it. "Your friend's on the outside of the ship."

"I know."

"How long has he got?"

From somewhere, a voice I don't know: *Fourteen.*

"Fourteen minutes," I say.

"That's plenty." He claps me once on the shoulder. Like he expects to see me again. He won't. "Go on, then. I was bad at standing around. You're not. Don't let it make you slow."

The table rattles. Not the table. A rail. Something hitting a rail, over and over, ringing.

Nobody else hears it.

---

Freja Lund. Medic. She takes my left arm without asking and turns it over on the table.

There's a hole through it. Clean. In one side, out the other. It doesn't hurt. That's wrong. I note it.

"That's going to scar," she says.

"Fine."

"It's going to stiffen up in the cold. You'll hold it close for a year and pretend you're not."

"Fine."

"You're going to say fine to everything I tell you."

"Yes."

She keeps hold of the arm. Her grip is warm. Warmer than the rest of me. Warmer than anything in here. She doesn't let go, even when I stop looking at her, and I understand it isn't her holding it.

Somebody, somewhere, is fixing it.

---

Han Wei. Lead scientist. Marcus Teague. Secondary scientist. Both at a whiteboard that wasn't there a second ago, arguing about a curve.

Teague sees me first.

"Him," he says. "The one in my seat. Vasek. Is he any good?"

"He talks through his math."

"Out loud?"

"Out loud. Every time."

Teague considers that. "Good. That's how you catch it." He goes back to the board. Then, without turning: "Tell him I'm not angry about the seat."

"He doesn't remember you."

"Tell him anyway."

Han Wei hasn't stopped writing. The handwriting gets faster and messier the further down the board it goes. I can't read it. I don't think it's meant to be read. I think it's what the inside of a room sounds like right before something goes wrong in it.

---

The end of the table is different.

Six people. Flight jackets, same as the others. The backup team.

Their name tags are blank. Not unwritten. Blank, like the dream never got the file.

I knew them. Not well. We passed in hallways. One of them held a door for me once with her tail, because her paws were full. One of them was always in the canteen before seven. That's all I've got. The adjacent wing. They were in the adjacent wing.

"We were next door," one of them says. Not to me. To nobody.

"I know."

"Nobody says our names."

"I don't know your names."

"No," he says. "That's what I mean."

I stay there longer than anywhere else. I don't have anything to give them. I stay anyway. Staying is a thing I can do.

When I get up, I count them. Six. I count again. Six.

That number holds. I don't know why that one does.

---

El'kian. Pilot. The one who made it as far as the beds. Grinning, one arm around somebody at the edge of the frame. Then the whole table is standing up, MK1 and everyone, in matching tracksuits, the ship behind us smaller than it has any right to look.

"Get in the shot properly," he says, off to the side, to Ethyn, who is reading a clipboard and not listening. "Come on. One good one."

Ethyn doesn't look up. El'kian puts an arm around his shoulder and grins at the camera anyway. The photo was his idea. Most of them were.

I'm not in it. I'm the one holding the camera.

I don't remember that until now.

---

Aria. Medical officer. She's sitting a little apart with a card in both paws. Block letters, uneven. DR MAMA. A drawing that might be a stethoscope and might be a snake.

"They're getting good at their letters," she says.

"Who?"

She turns the card over once. Twice. Checks something she already knows is fine.

"Somebody small," she says. "You've got one too."

I don't answer that.

There's someone at the very edge of the canteen. Small. Sitting on the floor with a crayon. I don't look straight at them. I know the angle you can look at this from, and how long you can hold it. I hold it for exactly that long.

A house. A figure with triangle ears and stick legs.

I stop.

---

A roof. Late light. A city underneath, gold.

Bejors is looking at the camera. Ethyn is looking at her.

I'm holding the camera. That one was me too.

Neither of them knew. She was mid-sentence. He'd stopped listening to the sentence. I took it because it was the only true thing on the roof and I wanted somebody to have it.

Bejors at a whiteboard, marker on her paws. "The Phengoclept doesn't care about our timelines. It's not waiting for us to catch up."

"Neither are you," Ethyn says.

"Someone has to not wait."

He laughs before he decides to. Head tipped back. She keeps talking through it.

I'm in the doorway. I don't go in. Some rooms are for two people.

Later, a hallway. Lights at half. Bejors on the floor outside the containment lab, knees up. Ethyn sits down beside her without being asked. Nobody says anything.

I walk past. They don't see me. That's correct.

---

Nelson's workbench. Metal shavings. A tool half apart. A paper in two columns, tallied.

"Sixty," he says to Ethyn, not looking up. "You're not getting out of this one."

"I don't even remember what for."

"You will. Or you won't."

He's grinning. Ethyn leaves. Nelson looks at me.

"What's it for?" I ask.

He goes back to the tool. Holds a paw out, palm up, without looking. I put the wrench in it. I always did. Some things you learn without being told.

"He'll find out," he says. "Or he won't."

---

The table again. Everyone at it. Main team, backup, MK1. Bowls going down the middle. Up, bowls, done.

I count.

Fifteen. Sixteen. Fourteen.

I need it to hold. I need one number in this room to hold still.

Somewhere very far off, not here: a man on the end of a tether. A clock going down from fourteen. A rail ringing every time something hits it.

I don't know which room that's from. It doesn't belong in this one.

The table starts to come apart.

Not loud. The far end goes first. The backup team's blank tags, then their faces, then the chairs. Then Han Wei's board, the writing sliding off it like it was never stuck on. Teague goes back to work. Freja's grip stays on my arm the longest, warm, then warmer, and I understand again that it isn't her.

Sari closes her laptop. That's the only time I ever saw her do it.

"Read the logs," she says.

Lev's gone. El'kian's gone. Aria puts the card in her pocket and is gone. Bejors and Ethyn on the roof, then just the roof, then just the light.

Nelson last. Same as he was. Book face-down, spine up. Coming back to it.

"You're back," he says.

"Yeah."

"Good."

That's the whole conversation. It's all it ever needed to be.

---

Something under all of it.

Low. A rhythm. Not a voice. Something mechanical, evenly spaced, built to be impossible to sleep through. It cuts through the canteen like a paw through water.

The light goes flat. The soup goes cold. The table goes.

I try to open my eyes. They don't. Something is pressing me down into the berth, harder than it should, which is the first wrong thing. The second is that something warmer than the rest of me has my left arm and isn't giving it back.

The alarm resolves into words.

"Contaminant detected."

Then another voice, closer, the same flat recorded calm.

"Mr. Kenzo. Remain in rest state. Treatment is incomplete. Dr. Vasek is being woken."

Ethyn. Awake. Alone with whatever that is.

I try to say his name. I get as far as the first letter.

"Rest well, Mr. Kenzo."

The static comes back, softer. Somewhere in it a paw is held out, palm up, and a wrench is placed in it, and nobody says another word about it.

I count one more time before I go under. Just the people I know are breathing.

Two.

It holds.

*END OF CHAPTER THIRTEEN*
