
There's a line for the food processor.

An actual line. Four of us clipped to the rail in the central hall, one behind the other in various stages of morning, like the world's most polite coat rack. In all the weeks I've been awake on this ship, nobody has ever had to wait for anything on it except bad news. I hook a boot on the rail at the back and take a moment to appreciate the novelty.

"This is wild," I say, to nobody in particular.

"What's wild," Petryn says, from the middle of the line. He's clipped himself to the rail by one boot and is turning slowly at the end of the strap, like a man waiting for a bus he has no strong feelings about.

"There's a line. For food. On my ship. I've never had to wait for anything on this ship except bad news."

"get used to it man," Maslin says, ahead of him, looking more awake than anyone should after that little sleep. "this is what mornings are like when its more than two people welcome to society"

"I liked it better when it was just Jinzhou and dread."

"I heard that," Jinzhou says, from the front of the line, not turning around.

The processor makes one tube at a time, about forty seconds each. Two people never noticed. Five people, forty seconds a tube: the line was never a possibility. It was math.

Maslin's turn comes. He turns the tube over, reads the label, and frowns at it.

"it says supplemental"

"Crew designation pending manifest update," ARACCSIM says, from the speaker over the processor.

"ive been demoted to a supplement"

"Could be worse," Petryn says, still turning. "Could say 'miscellaneous.'"

---

My own tube comes out last, stamped VASEK, and I carry it to Lab A, where Marsh has been up for an hour and has eaten hers with a napkin clipped under it, for reasons she hasn't shared. I don't ask.

"ARACCSIM," I say. "Updated supply math. Short version, and I'm going to pretend I didn't hear the long one."

"Combined supply projection, accounting for salvaged MK2 rations and five crew on the current sleep rotation, yields arrival with a margin of forty-one days."

Forty-one. I check it against the trip home, a little over a thousand days, and get four percent. "That's a thin margin. I've seen wider margins on a paperback."

"Confirmed workable, within acceptable margin," ARACCSIM says, which from it is roughly a gold star.

It's thin. It's also positive, and right now positive is a good enough word to build a morning on.

Marsh looks up from the far end of the bench.

"Forty-one," she repeats, quietly, checking it against whatever ledger she's been keeping since the first time we talked logistics. "I had thirty-eight."

"I may have been generous with my assumptions last night," I admit. "In my defense, I wanted breakfast to feel like good news for once."

"That's not very scientifically rigorous of you, Ethyn."

"No," I agree. "But it is very me. I'm keeping the three days."

"They're provisional days."

"Those are the best kind."

She almost smiles. Actually almost, the corner of her mouth trying something it clearly doesn't try often, and I decide not to make a big deal of noticing, because I've learned that much about how to be around her.

---

By midmorning the labs are a mess. Five people working in rooms built for two, and in weightlessness a crowd is three-dimensional. Marsh has taken the wall station next to Jinzhou in Lab A and is working upside down relative to him, and neither of them has brought it up. It should feel chaotic in a bad way. Somehow it doesn't.

Petryn has found a corner of Lab C and colonized it with quiet competence, running diagnostics on the salvaged water reclamation parts with the same patience he brought to that dead terminal on MK2, humming something I don't recognize and don't ask about.

Marsh and Jinzhou are cross-referencing fuel curves in a silence that isn't awkward so much as companionable, two people who communicate mostly through shared spreadsheets and the occasional precise correction. Marsh has a second spreadsheet open that, as far as I can tell, exists to check the first.

Maslin is underfoot everywhere, which I'm coming to understand is just who he is, restless energy that needs somewhere to go. Today it's going into the supply cabinet. He spent four months rationing scraps and is having real trouble believing in a cabinet that isn't empty.

"you dont need eight backup thermal blankets," he says, to nobody, pulling them out one at a time. Each one drifts off to join the others in a slow silver cloud, and he keeps swatting them back without looking. "who packed this. who thought eight was a reasonable number"

"Contingency planning," I say. "Argon loves contingency planning."

"argon loves makin other people carry their contingency planning is what argon loves"

He stacks the blankets anyway, careful despite the complaint, and I notice he doesn't actually get rid of any of them.

---

By evening the ship feels different in a way I don't have a precise word for. Fuller. Louder, in places that were never loud. Somewhere near storage, Maslin and Petryn are disagreeing about cable routing, animated on Maslin's end and completely unbothered on Petryn's. It's been going for forty minutes. Neither of them is trying to win it.

I pass the dorm on the way to the cockpit. The duffel is strapped into its berth, belt across the middle, like a passenger who's nervous about turbulence. It's currently the best-behaved member of the crew.

Jinzhou finds me in the cockpit, where I've retreated for a few minutes of something like quiet.

"Long day," he says.

"Good day," I say. "Just loud."

He drifts into the co-pilot seat and buckles in, two clicks, no wasted motion, and looks at the screens. The star map is gone. In its place is the trajectory home, and a distance number that only goes one direction now.

"Five people," he says. "I keep counting."

"How many times today?"

"Eleven."

"And?"

"It was five every time."

I look at him. He doesn't look back, which is how I know he's pleased with that one.

"Does it feel different? Than six?"

"I don't remember six," he says. "Not really. But I assume it wasn't this."

I don't know what to say to that, so I don't say anything. We sit there a while, the ship humming around us, fuller than it's been since launch, quieter than it was an hour ago, heading slowly, carefully, toward something that might actually be home.

*END OF CHAPTER TWENTY-TWO*
