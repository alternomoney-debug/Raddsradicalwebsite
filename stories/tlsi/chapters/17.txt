
The knock comes back three times. Evenly spaced. The same rhythm we gave it.

So either whoever's in there has a sense of humor, or they've run out of better ideas too.

"Somebody's alive in there," I say. To nobody. To the bulkhead. To the enormous fact that there's a heartbeat out here besides mine and Jinzhou's. "That's the first thing out here that's ever knocked back!"

"Confirmed," Jinzhou says.

That's maybe the least necessary confirmation he's ever given me. I heard it too. But I'm glad he said it. Said out loud, it's a thing that happened to both of us. Not something I made up after five months of wanting it.

I don't try the wheel again. Not yet. Why not? Because whoever's in there sealed this door on purpose. If I were locked in a dead ship and two strangers in suits started pounding on my door, I wouldn't open it either. I'd write a will.

"ARACCSIM," I say, backing off from the bulkhead. "Talk to me. What do we actually know about this ship?"

"Processing."

Ugh. I hate that word right now. I know that's not fair. It's a computer asking me to wait. But waiting is the one thing I've got no patience left for. Not with a stranger's heartbeat on the other side of a door.

"How long is processing?"

"Unspecified. Data recovery from a passive burst is not instantaneous."

"That's not an answer. That's a shrug wearing a badge."

"Noted, Dr. Vasek."

I don't think it can do sarcasm. And yet.

---

We drift back to the storage room while ARACCSIM works. Staring at a sealed door isn't helping anybody, and I need somewhere to hook a foot that isn't directly in front of the thing scaring me.

The plants are still there. Still green. I look at them longer than I need to. It's easier than looking at the empty berths through the doorway. Easier than looking at Jinzhou, who's holding very still. Holding something in.

"You okay?" I ask. It seems like the thing to ask, even if I don't expect a real answer.

"Define okay."

"Fair. Let me rephrase. Are you functional for the next ten minutes?"

He unclips one of the pots and turns it a little. Like he's checking the soil. Or checking something else and using the soil as an excuse to give his paws a job. He runs a finger along the wick. The membrane. The water bag underneath.

"Capillary feed," he says. "No pump, no gravity. The water climbs the wick by itself. It is good work." He's quiet for a moment. "Somebody kept these alive. Whatever else happened here. Somebody decided this mattered enough to keep doing."

"Somebody who's still watering on a schedule," I say. "I couldn't manage that in a good year."

"That is a person who has not given up. Whoever it is."

I look at him.

"You're doing the thing," I say, "where you say something that sounds like it's about the plants, and it's actually about something else."

"I do not know what you mean."

"Sure you don't."

He clips the pot back exactly where it was. Careful. Precise. Same as everything. He doesn't say anything else about it. I've decided that's its own kind of answer.

"ARACCSIM. Transponder data. Lead with anything that isn't bad news."

"Vessel designation confirmed via hull transponder data, recovered from short-range telemetry burst upon docking. Vanguard MK2. Launch date approximately four months following Vanguard MK1 departure from Wask-ki orbit."

"Four months. So it's younger than us. Barely."

"Correct, in terms of construction date. Vessel classification: cargo and resupply configuration. Crew complement: three."

"Cargo," Jinzhou says. "Not a science mission."

"Correct. Original mission parameters listed in transponder data indicate a resupply and support role for Vanguard MK1, with extended-duration capability."

Resupply. For MK1.

For us.

"They were coming to help us," I say. "That's what this was. Not a second try at the same mission. A supply run. For us!"

"Available data is consistent with that interpretation."

Wait.

There's a number I've been carrying around since my first week awake. And it just stood up in the back of my head and cleared its throat.

"ARACCSIM. Our original food. Say it again."

"Vanguard MK1 was provisioned for six crew across a projected ninety-day mission window, plus fifteen percent contingency reserve."

"Ninety days."

"Correct."

"For a trip that was always going to take years each way."

"The crew was to spend the transit in rest state. Provisioning applied to waking periods only."

"Ninety days awake." I look at Jinzhou. "Six people. Ninety days in orbit at Asimov, plus a little extra in case somebody dropped a tube. That was the plan. That was the whole food plan."

"It was a short plan," Jinzhou says.

"It was a short plan for a long mission! I noticed it in week one and decided it was somebody else's problem, because I was busy learning my own name."

Okay. Work it out. Why would you only pack ninety days?

Ninety days is enough if everything goes perfectly. Wake up. Collect. Go home. But nothing about this mission was ever going to go perfectly. Somebody at Argon knew that. So instead of packing more food on our ship...

"They built a second one," I say.

"A supply ship," Jinzhou says.

"A supply ship! Launched four months after us. To meet us out here with everything we'd run out of. The pantry was always going to show up later." I let go of the rail without meaning to and have to grab it again. "That's not a backup plan. That is the plan. They planned for us to need it. And they didn't tell us."

"They may have told Bejors."

"Maybe." I think about the server room. The archive. The folder with her name on it that I've never opened. "Maybe they told the people who were supposed to wake up first. And the people who were supposed to wake up first didn't."

Jinzhou doesn't answer that. He doesn't have to.

So I count. Counting helps. Four of us died in the beds. Their food sat on the shelves and fed the two of us instead. That's the only reason we've lasted this long. And somewhere on this ship, behind a door that knocks back, are three people whose whole job was to bring us more.

"So if they hadn't come," I say slowly, "and if four of us hadn't died. All six of us would have run out of food at Asimov in about three months."

"Approximately," ARACCSIM says. "Yes."

"I hate that," I tell it. "I hate that specifically. Put it in a log."

"Logged, Dr. Vasek."

"Thank you."

It doesn't help. But it's on the record. That's more than Argon gave us.

"What can you tell me about what happened to them? How they're doing now."

A pause. Longer than I want.

"Limited. The transponder burst contains only vessel registration and launch-configuration data. Original crew manifest, cargo listing, mission parameters as designed. This data is static. It reflects the vessel's condition and intent at launch, not its current state."

"So you can tell me what this ship was supposed to be. Not what it is now."

"Correct."

"Huh. That's a résumé, not a person," I say. "You can read somebody's résumé all day and have no idea what kind of morning they're having."

"I do not have a frame of reference for that comparison."

"You're not missing much."

Okay. Next question. Can we just talk to them?

"Comms. Real ones. Can we talk to whoever's behind that door?"

"Negative. No functioning communication array detected aboard Blip-D. The transponder is a passive registration beacon, not a communication system. It broadcasts static identification data regardless of the vessel's operational status. There is no channel available for direct exchange."

"So we've got their number in the phone book," I say, "and the number goes to a disconnected line."

"That is a reasonably accurate characterization," Jinzhou says, with a weird little burst of something almost like excitement. Gone again as fast as it showed up. He does that sometimes. Little flashes of feeling, gone before I can react.

I don't say anything about it. I've learned not to.

"Names," I say. "Does the manifest at least give us names?"

"Manifest data available. Corwin, M. Quartermaster. Okonjo, M. Pilot. Draven, P. Engineer."

I say them out loud. Slowly. Like I already know they're going to matter.

"Corwin. Okonjo. Draven."

Three people. Launched four months after us, on a ship built to help us. And it ended up out here, torn open and dark and drifting, with one room still sealed and defended from the inside.

I remember waking up on our ship with no idea who I was. Just a name on a food tube, and whatever the arms decided to feed me. Three more people went through some version of that out here. Except theirs came with whatever ripped those tanks off, instead of a coma fog. I don't know yet which one's worse.

Then something else clicks.

"They don't know about us," I say, working it out loud. "I mean, they knew we existed. They knew they were coming to resupply MK1. But they don't know what happened to us. They don't know four of our six are dead. They don't know it's been just me and Jinzhou this whole time."

"Unknown," ARACCSIM says. Correctly. It can't tell what three strangers behind a door do or don't know, and I appreciate that it doesn't pretend.

"And we do not know what happened to them," Jinzhou says. "Three crew launched. We do not know if three crew are still alive in there."

"No," I say. "We don't."

---

We go back to the bulkhead.

I hang in front of it for a while before I do anything. There's a version of the next few minutes where this goes well, and a version where it doesn't. I can't tell which one I'm braced for.

"I'm going to try the wheel," I say. "Slow. Give them time to see it moving, if they're watching."

"Go," Jinzhou says.

I brace a foot on the rail and put both paws on it. It's stiff. Not locked. Just stiff, like nobody's turned it in way too long. Not pressure-sealed like the first door. This one just needs convincing.

A quarter turn. I stop. I wait.

Nothing pushes back from the other side.

Another quarter turn.

"Anybody in there?" I call, loud enough to carry through the metal. "We're not, uh. We're not hostile. We're the Vanguard MK1. We've been stuck out here as long as you have, probably. We just want to talk."

Nothing for a second.

Then, muffled and far away, definitely a voice. Definitely a person. I can't make out the words through the door, but I can hear the shape of talking. Somebody saying something back. Fast. Urgent.

I turn the wheel the rest of the way.

The door gives with a groan that sounds less like a machine and more like the ship letting out a breath it's been holding for a long, long time.

It swings in.

The first thing I see, past the edge of the door, past the dim emergency light, is a shape kicking away from us fast. Curled up tight. Defensive. Whoever it is has been practicing the worst version of this moment for a long time.

"Whoa. Hey. Hey, easy," I say, paws up. That feels ridiculous in an EVA suit. Apparently it's just what my body does. "We're not, we're friendly, we're..."

"youre MK1," the shape says. A voice. Rough from not being used, but sharp under that. Quick. A voice that talks faster than it thinks and doesn't much care.

"Yeah, MK1," I say. "Vanguard, if you want the long version. I wouldn't recommend the long version."

A long pause. The shape hasn't come any closer. But it hasn't backed off any farther either. I'm counting that as progress.

"how many of you," the voice asks.

"Two," I say. "Just two. It's, uh. It's a long story. Two of us made it."

"there were six of you," the voice says. Not quite a question.

"Six," I say. "I was asleep for most of the subtracting."

"and now theres two"

"And now there's two," I agree. "It's a number I really want to stop saying out loud."

Another pause. Longer. Somebody in there deciding something big.

"how do i know youre actually who you say you are-" the voice asks. Stops. Starts over. "instead of, i dunno, instead of whatever else couldve been wearin a suit like that"

It's a dumb question. I don't have an answer ready. It never once occurred to me that the hard part of all this would be getting believed. And something in me, tired and worn down and a long way from home, just snaps.

"WHO ELSE WOULD HAVE THESE SUITS?" I yell. I can hear myself doing it and I can't stop. "ARGON ONLY SEN- ONLY SENT UP MK ONE AND, I GUESS, TWO! I'VE GOT A SHIP PARKED OUTSIDE YOUR HULL WITH OUR NAMES ON THE TRANSPONDER! WE HAVE THEE JINZHOU!"

Silence.

I'm breathing hard. I did not mean to do that. I don't think I've yelled like that since the day I woke up without my name. I hang there in the doorway, dumb and furious and totally unable to take any of it back.

Jinzhou, next to me, doesn't say anything. Under the circumstances, that feels like the right call.

Then, from inside, a laugh.

Short. Surprised. Real.

"ok-" the voice says, "okay yeah fair enough- nobody fakes an accent like that"

"I don't have an accent," I say. Still too loud. Still coming down off whatever that was.

"everybodys got an accent to somebody" the voice says. "you can"

And it stops there. Like the sentence ran out before it figured out what it was offering.

You can come in. You can trust us. You can put your paws down.

I don't know which one it means. From the way it trails off, I don't think they know either.

*END OF CHAPTER SEVENTEEN*
