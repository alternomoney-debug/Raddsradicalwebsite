
The arms lower him to the floor when his legs don't hold. He lets them. He ends up flat on his back, staring at the ceiling. Or possibly at the sock.

I slide down the wall and sit next to him. My heart takes its time coming back from wherever it went.

Neither of us talks for a while.

Then he does. Still looking up. The end of it lifts, so I'm pretty sure it's a question. It isn't in any language I know.

"*Itu kaus kakimu?*"

"Sorry?"

He turns his head and looks at me. Then at the sock. Then at me. He points at it.

"Oh! Yes. That's mine. We had a, there was a disagreement. The arm won. On points."

He looks at me the way you'd look at a radio picking up the wrong station. Then he goes back to the ceiling.

He didn't get a word of that.

There's a logo on the wall panel beside us. ARGON INDUSTRIES / LSI DIVISION. His paw finds it without him looking. Traces the letters once. Stops.

"*Kenapa saya ingat mereka?*" he says. Not to me.

I don't know what it means. I know what it sounds like. Something flickers behind his eyes, and whatever it is, he doesn't let it finish.

And I know the sound of the words, too. Not the meaning. The shape. Tall, narrow sounds, an unfamiliar rhythm. The exact rhythm of the menus I couldn't read upstairs.

He isn't just speaking some language I don't have. He's speaking the ship.

I get up and offer him a paw. He takes it. Once he's standing, he taps his own chest.

"*Saya...*" He stops. Frowns. Like the word is right there, on the wrong side of a door. When it comes, it comes slowly, one syllable at a time, like furniture going through a narrow hallway. "Engineer. I am... engineer."

"Ethyn," I say, and tap my own chest, since apparently that's how we're doing this. "Ethyn. Vasek. Scientist."

"Etin."

"Close enough! Do you speak, uh. English?"

He thinks about it. Then he holds up a thumb and finger with almost no space between them.

"Little."

"Okay. Little's more than I have of yours."

---

He looks at me for a solid minute without saying anything. Then he walks to a console across the room, one I'd walked past twice without noticing. In my defense, I was busy yelling at furniture. One line of amber text on a dark screen.

LSI-ASM&C / STANDBY

"*Kita di mana?*" he says to the screen.

I hold up my sleeve. "I worked some of it out. Upstairs. It's not, it's not great news."

He looks at the numbers. He doesn't ask. He taps something, and the screen fills up with text, and he starts reading to himself, lips moving.

"It's in your language," I say. "Isn't it. All the screens."

He nods. "*Bahasa Indonesia.*"

"Can you read it to me? In mine?"

He tries. He reads a line to himself, then goes hunting for the English, and what he brings back is pieces.

"Long Shot... Initiative. Vanguard." A long stretch to himself. "Welcome. You are... awake."

In the bottom corner of the screen, a notification pops up in plain capitals even I can read.

A.R.A.C.C.S.I.M. READY TO SWITCH WITH LSI-ASM&C.

He reads more. A whole paragraph goes by in his language. When he comes up for air, what he's got for me is: "Crew. Here." He points at the beds. "This is... crew."

A.R.A.C.C.S.I.M. SWITCHING. PREPARING HANDOVER.

He goes down the list with one finger on the screen and his other paw pointing around the room, one bed at a time.

"*Pilot.*" Row one, top bed. The big wolf I knocked on like a door. "*Serigala.* Big. Wolf."

A.R.A.C.C.S.I.M. HANDOVER IN PROGRESS.

"*Dokter.*" Middle bed. The squashed ears. He searches. "Doctor. Cat... doctor."

"*Insinyur mesin.*" Bottom bed, row one. The jaw. "Engineer. Machine." He reads a little further, and something almost happens to his face. "*Selalu marah.*" He does an impression. It's a scowl. It's a really good scowl. "Always... angry."

I laugh. I don't mean to. It comes out sideways and surprises both of us.

Next line. He reads it, stops, and taps his own chest twice with two fingers. That's all. He doesn't need the English for that one.

"*Kepala ilmuwan.*" The vulpes in my row. The one I called ma'am. "Science. First."

So she's the lead scientist. I knew she'd know things. I'd really like her to wake up so I can tell her I called it.

"*Ilmuwan kedua.*" He looks at me. Then, without any warning at all, he reaches over and taps the frame of my glasses with one claw. "*Kacamata.*"

"Glasses," I say.

"Glasses," he repeats. He reads a little more. "If glasses. Fine."

"I have my glasses," I say. "I'm not fine. For the record."

He doesn't get that either. He keeps going.

A.R.A.C.C.S.I.M. NOW OPERATING LSI VESSEL.

Then nothing. It just sits there in the background, humming, like it's been waiting the whole time and has no feelings about finally being noticed.

He reads the last line. "*Semoga berhasil.*" Then, carefully, in English, like he's setting down something that might break: "Good. Luck."

I look around the room. The pilot, the doctor, the machine engineer, the lead scientist. All asleep. Two of us awake, one language between us, and he's only got a little of it.

One of the arms drops from the ceiling and taps him on the shoulder. It's holding a tube. He takes it, reads the label, and eats the whole thing. No argument. Tube up, tube down, tube in his pocket. Then he holds up the label so I can see it, without being asked. The labels, at least, are in capitals I can read.

KENZO, J. / SOFTWARE ENGINEER
MEAL: 2/3 / DAY: 1

Meal two. He's been awake for five minutes and he's already a meal ahead of me.

"Kenzo," I say.

He nods. "Kenzo."

"What's the J for?"

He thinks about it. I watch him think about it, and I watch him find nothing, and I know that face. I wore it all morning.

"*Tidak tahu,*" he says, and shrugs, and I get that one without any help at all.

---

The ladder's easier the second time. Not easy. Easier.

He goes up first. He doesn't ask about the whiteboard when we pass it, or the lab, or the console with the camera feed still glowing on it. He looks at all of it once, left to right, like he's filing it, and keeps climbing.

At the top he stops below the hatch and looks at me. I climb past him, which is a strange thing to do on a ladder, but he moves aside without being asked.

IDENTIFY TO PROCEED.

"Ethyn Vasek," I say. Second time today. I'm getting good at this.

A pause. Longer than I'd like.

"Dr. Vasek."

It's a different voice. Smoother. Less like a vending machine and more like a vending machine that went to college. And my name comes out wrong. It's in a different tone from the rest of the sentence, flat, like somebody recorded my surname once and the computer has to paste that same clip in every time, whether it fits or not.

"Identity confirmed. Who is your co-pilot? Please state their name."

This time I have one. I look down at him.

"Engineer Kenzo," I say. "I trust him. He read me a list. Mostly in his language, but he read it."

"Mr. Kenzo's identity is partially confirmed. First name required."

"He doesn't remember it."

"That information is available to confirmed crew only."

"I am confirmed crew. You just said so. In a weird voice, but you said it."

A pause.

"Jinzhou," it says. "Mr. Jinzhou T. Kenzo."

I look back down the ladder. At Kenzo. At Jinzhou.

"Jinzhou," I say.

He tries it. "Jinzhou Kenzo." Then, quieter, just to himself, like he's checking a measurement: "Jinzhou."

The hatch clicks. I climb up first and take the left seat. He comes through after me and takes the right.

Most of the console in front of me is in the same tall, narrow lettering as the lab. There's a strip of switches along the bottom edge of my side, worn smooth like they get used a lot. Out of habit, I mouth the words on them. Sounds with nothing behind them.

Jinzhou glances at the switches, and for the first time since he sat up, he looks completely at home.

"What is the purpose of this mission," the new voice asks.

I open my mouth. Close it. Something comes up, slow and certain, the way my name did.

"To stop Nakuet from dimming," I say. "To save Wask-ki from freezing."

A pause. Longer than the question needs.

"Partial confirm. Dr. Vasek, you are cleared as secondary pilot. Do you vouch for Mr. Kenzo as co-pilot."

"Yes."

"Jinzhou T. Kenzo." His name comes out in a different tone too, but warmer. A little higher. Like it was recorded on a better day by somebody in a better mood. "Co-pilot access granted. Welcome aboard, you two. ARACCSIM is now at your service."

"ARACCSIM," I say. "That's you."

"Correct."

"Talking to you is like talking to a Spell'n'Speak," I say. "It's, I don't love it."

Jinzhou points at the speaker grille in the ceiling. Then at his own ear. "It... hear."

"I know. That's why I said 'I don't love it' and not something worse."

"Noted," ARACCSIM says, in what sounds like the voice of a third, friendlier machine.

How many voices does this thing have?

I pull up the navigation screen. I already know what it's going to say. I want to see it anyway. The star map is centered on a point labeled ASI-06A, with a trajectory line running from somewhere far to the left all the way to where we are now. There's a label in the top right corner, in the lettering I can't read.

"Jinzhou," I say. "Can you read that? Please?"

He reads it to himself first. "*Sistem saat ini: Asimov. Jarak dari sistem Nakuet: enam koma empat tahun cahaya.*"

Then he goes looking for my words and brings back what he can carry. "Now. System. Asimov." He holds up six fingers. Makes a little dot in the air. Holds up four. "Light. Year. From Nakuet."

Six point four.

I had the shape of it. Now the shape has a number, and the number is worse, because you can't round a number down just because you'd like to.

Six point four light-years. Light from home takes six years and change to get here. And whatever got us here wasn't going anywhere near light speed, I don't think, so it took us longer than that. A lot longer. So I've been asleep for years. Plural.

That explains the ports.

I hold out my sleeve to him. He reads the columns of timestamps going up to my elbow, and the thirty-two at the bottom, circled three times.

"Rotation period," I say. "I did it upstairs. Three spots. That isn't our star."

"No," he says. "It is not."

"ARACCSIM. Confirm."

"Stephfren Asimov Memorial System, designation ASI-06A. You are currently inbound within the Asimov system. Approximate time to Asimov-09D is about one week."

I let out a breath I've been holding since the lab.

"I know," I say. "I just wanted somebody else to say it."

---

"Partial confirm," it turns out, is a phrase ARACCSIM likes a lot more than I do.

"What does partial mean?" I ask. "Partial like eighty percent? Or partial like you're being polite about a D minus?"

"Mission purpose response was substantively correct. Additional detail withheld pending full crew confirmation."

"Withheld by who?"

"By design specification."

"That's not an answer. That's a sentence shaped like an answer. It's wearing an answer's clothes."

"Correct."

I look at Jinzhou, half hoping for backup, but he's already moved on. He's scrolling through something on the main screen with the flat focus of a man who has decided that arguing with the ship is a waste of daylight. (There is no daylight. We're in a can, six point four light-years from home.)

"It's fine," I say, mostly to myself. "We'll, uh, get the rest eventually."

"*Mungkin,*" Jinzhou says.

"I don't know what that means, but it sounded a lot like 'or we won't.' Thank you. Very helpful."

He shrugs, mostly with his ears.

I go back to my screen. There's a number on it I keep coming back to. Nakuet's dimming. Sixteen point two percent.

That's huge. That's not a weather problem. Sixteen percent less sunlight means crops don't grow, rivers don't thaw. That's a whole planet getting colder, every year, a little faster.

I hate that number. It's also the only thing on this ship that hasn't been vague with me once. So I do the only thing that helps when a number won't leave me alone. I work.

"ARACCSIM. Show me the full luminosity curve. Not the summary. Every data point you've got."

A pause. Longer than I'd expect from something that thinks in numbers I can't count to.

"Working."

The screen fills with a graph. Hundreds of points. A long, mostly flat line for years and years, and then, near the end, a curve that bends downward like it's tired of holding itself up.

"That's not steady," I say.

"No," Jinzhou agrees.

"It's speeding up."

"Yes."

I trace the curve with the stylus. I don't need to. But part of my brain only trusts information once my paw has touched it, which is an inconvenient way to be built when your whole job is on a screen.

If the dimming were steady, you'd get a straight line, going down. This isn't straight. It's a curve that gets steeper the further you go. Every year it loses more than the year before. That's what you'd expect from something that's growing. Something alive, making more of itself, eating more as there's more of it.

"If it keeps speeding up like this," I say, "give me the days to critical."

Jinzhou pulls the number without being asked twice. He doesn't try to say it. He just turns the screen toward me. Numbers are numbers.

Two thousand one hundred.

"Days," I say. "That's days."

He nods.

I do the conversion out loud, because it's easier that way. "Two thousand one hundred days. Divide by, uh, three sixty-five. That's... five point seven-ish. Under six years."

"Yes."

"That's not the number on the whiteboard downstairs."

"No," he says. "It is not."

"Fudge," I say to the console.

The cockpit hums around us. Small fans, distant machinery. A room built to keep two people alive somewhere that would rather they weren't.

"ARACCSIM," I say. "Why weren't we told the rate was speeding up before we left?"

"That information was classified under mission need-to-know protocols."

"We're the mission."

"Confirmed crew. Not full crew."

I glare at the ceiling. I say "fudge" again. Twice. With feeling.

"There it is again," I say.

"Yes," Jinzhou says.

---

We keep at it for a while. Fuel curves. Trajectory windows. How much braking we need to arrive at Asimov-09D. It feels productive right up until I notice we've been at it for two hours and I still don't know why we're here. Only how we got here, and how fast the clock is ticking. Two hours, zero answers, one headache. Great first day.

"ARACCSIM," I say. "Run a full system diagnostic. I want to know what's actually working before I trust another number from you."

"Diagnostic initiated."

The screens cycle through a long list of subsystems, each one blinking green as it clears. Phengomotor housing. Comms array. Hull integrity. Life support. Down the list it goes, calm and thorough, in the voice of someone reading a grocery list they stopped caring about years ago.

"No anomalies detected," it says.

The word lands somewhere behind my eyes and stays there.

Anomaly.

"Ethyn."

I don't answer. I'm not entirely in the room anymore.

---

My office. I know it by feel, not sight. The chair has a lean to it I've never fixed, mostly out of spite for whoever bought these chairs in bulk without checking that any of them sat level.

Y'shari's voice comes through the speakerphone badly, the signal dropping every few words, wherever she's calling from.

"Say that again," I tell her. "Slower. You're explaining the feeding cycle like I've read the same papers you have."

"You have read the same papers."

"I skimmed the abstracts. That's different, and you know it's different, and I don't like that you keep pretending it isn't."

She sighs. Over a bad line it's mostly static, with feeling.

"Fine. The anomaly." Papers shuffle on her end. "Every system we've mapped follows the same pattern. Line appears, star dims, line gets brighter, star dims faster. Every single one. Except ASI-06A."

"What's different?"

"The line's there. Full strength. It hasn't grown or faded in two years of watching. But the star isn't dimming. Not even a fraction of a percent."

I lean back. The chair creaks its usual complaint.

"So the light's being taken, but nothing's actually being lost? That's, uh. That's not how stealing works, Y'shari. Stealing means something ends up missing."

"That's the theory."

"That doesn't make sense."

"Welcome to my week."

I pull my notepad closer and start writing in shorthand. Y'shari has told me twice to just type my drafts. She's right. I don't, because typing feels like admitting the notepad lost, and I'm not ready to let the notepad lose.

"Walk me through it," I say. "If the arc's, um, stable instead of migrating. What would that mean?"

"Two possibilities. One, whatever's out there stopped moving. Found a stable population and stayed."

"And two?"

"Two is worse."

"Everything with you is worse lately. You've turned into a doom prophet. It's not a good look."

"Two is that something's keeping it in balance. Something eating it back as fast as it grows. A predator, maybe. The system found an equilibrium on its own, instead of getting stripped bare like everywhere else."

I stop writing. Read back what I've got. Equilibrium. I underline it twice, so hard the pen nearly goes through the page.

"If that's true," I say, "that's not just interesting. That's the whole thing. That's the answer."

"I know."

"That's not a study guide chapter, Y'shari. That's a reason to build a ship."

Silence. A beat too long.

"They're already talking about it," she says, quieter. "Not officially. But they're talking."

"About building a ship to go look."

"About who they'd send."

I laugh, because the alternative is not laughing, and not laughing feels premature.

"Good thing that's not my problem."

She doesn't laugh back.

I should have noticed that. I didn't.

"I have to go," she says. "But sit with this before you write anything. It's not settled science. It's a working theory built on one weird system and a lot of hope."

"Hope with citations."

"Something like that. Talk soon."

The line goes dead. I sit there staring at the word equilibrium, underlined twice. I have no idea I'm looking at my own future.

---

"Ethyn."

Jinzhou's voice, closer now, and flatter. It's the flat he uses when he's said my name more than once and is starting to wonder if I've turned into furniture.

I blink. The cockpit comes back. The screens. The green checkmarks. Jinzhou's face, mildly expectant. Probably the same face he'd make if the ship were on fire.

"You go," he says. He flicks two fingers away from the side of his head. "Somewhere. Again."

"Yeah." I push my glasses up. "Good one this time, though! Actually useful. Not just sad for no reason."

"Good?"

"Really good." I sit up straighter. "ASI-06A isn't a random stop. The line here doesn't grow like every other one they've mapped. Something's keeping it stable. In, uh. Balance. Equilibrium."

I can see him lose me halfway through "equilibrium." So I try again, with my paws. One paw is the thing eating the star. The other paw is the thing eating it back. I bring them together and hold them level.

He looks at my paws for a long time.

"We... take it," he says. "Home?"

"I think so. I think that's the job."

He's quiet for a second, which for Jinzhou counts as a big reaction.

"Big job," he says.

"Big job." I rub my eyes under my glasses. "And my brain's handing out memories whenever it feels like it. I don't get to pick. If I did, I'd schedule them for breakfast. Somewhere with good lighting. Not in a cockpit in the middle of a math problem."

He lets that stand. From him, that's agreement.

---

Neither of us wants to sit in the cockpit chewing on how big the problem is, so we go looking for something to do with our paws. That turns out to mean exploring the rest of the ship.

Back down through the lab shaft, past the whiteboard. Partway down, Jinzhou stops at a hatch set into the wall that I hadn't noticed, half hidden behind a rack of strapped-down gear.

"What's that?"

"*Tidak tahu,*" he says. That's his answer to most things, I'm learning, but he says it while already prying at the release latch, so not knowing doesn't seem to be slowing him down.

The hatch gives with a hiss of equalizing pressure. Beyond it is a short passage, low ceiling, lockers on both sides. Little embossed nameplates on each one.

"Dormitory annex," ARACCSIM offers, without being asked, which is new. "Personal storage. You are cleared for access to your own compartment. Crewmate compartments require owner authorization or confirmed incapacitation."

"Confirmed incapacitation," I repeat. Saying it out loud makes it worse.

"Correct."

Neither of us pushes on that. Some doors you open slowly.

I find mine near the end. VASEK, E., stenciled in block letters. Same font as the food tubes. Same font Argon seems to use for everything, from meal packs to lockers to, probably, somebody's headstone somewhere. My paw hovers over the latch.

Jinzhou taps the latch and raises his eyebrows. "Open?"

"I'm having a moment. Give me a second."

He doesn't understand the words. He waits anyway. That's better.

I open it.

Not much inside. A folded set of civilian clothes. A small stack of actual paper, handwritten notes in a cramped scrawl. My scrawl, I assume, though I don't remember writing any of it. So I'm a man of few possessions and bad handwriting. Noted.

And, wedged into the back corner, like somebody snuck it past inspection: a single, slightly crushed box. On the lid, in handwriting that is definitely not mine, in loopy cursive:

FOR WHEN YOU FORGET HOW TO HAVE FUN. -P.

There's a little heart where the period after the P should be. Drawn without thinking, like a habit.

Inside are six dried fruit bars, individually wrapped. Real food. Solid food! Not tube gravy.

Six bars. I run the rationing math. The math recommends discipline. I ignore the math.

"*Apa?*" Jinzhou says.

"Snacks. Somebody smuggled me snacks." He doesn't know "smuggled." I mime it, badly. A box going under a coat. He nods slowly, the way you'd nod at a crime.

"They hid it well enough that ARACCSIM didn't catch it during inventory. Which means either Argon's quality control is bad, or this person is really, really good at crime. And they signed it P, and I think." I stop. Something warm and stupid comes up in my chest. "I think I know exactly who that is."

I don't, actually. Not yet. But my chest does.

"This is the best thing that's happened to me since I woke up," I say. "And I'm including finding out my name."

Jinzhou opens his own locker. No ceremony. He stares into it for a long moment.

"Kenzo," he reads off the nameplate, mostly to himself. Inside: folded gray clothes, same as mine. And one object, sitting dead center, like somebody placed it there on purpose.

A small square of paper, folded so many times the creases have gone soft.

He opens it slowly, with the kind of care you give something you already know matters. It's a crayon drawing. A shape that might be a house. And a shape that might be him, if you squint. All stick legs and triangle ears. But unmistakably him.

No signature. It doesn't need one.

"*Saya tidak mengerti,*" he says, very quietly.

"I don't know what that means," I say. "I think I know what it means. You'll get there. Give it time." I hold up one of the fruit bars, point at the drawing, then point at the bar again. A trade. Snack for the story.

He looks at the bar. Looks at the drawing. Folds it back along its own creases, carefully, like it might tear, and puts it back in the locker. Then he takes the bar.

"*Oke,*" he says. Then, carefully, in mine: "Drawing... stays."

"I wasn't asking for the drawing. I'm not a monster."

He doesn't understand that. He keeps the drawing anyway.

We sit there for a minute in the narrow little passage, eating smuggled morale food six point four light-years from whoever packed it. And for the first time since I woke up not knowing my own name, something in my chest loosens. Just a little. Just enough to notice.

"*Dua ribu seratus,*" Jinzhou says eventually, out of nowhere, chewing.

I don't speak a word of his language. I still know exactly which number that was.

"Please don't ruin the snack. I was having a moment."

He holds up the bar. "*Enak.*" He almost smiles. It's a tiny thing, barely a shift in his whiskers, but I see it. Then, in English, very carefully, like he's handing me a present: "Good. Bar."

"Told you."

Somewhere above us, ARACCSIM logs the moment, in whatever way ARACCSIM logs things. It files it right next to the fuel percentages and the diagnostic checkmarks, like it's just as important.

At this point, it might be.

*END OF CHAPTER TWO*
