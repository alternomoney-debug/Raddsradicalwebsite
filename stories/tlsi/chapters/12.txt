
Warm.

That's the first thing. Just warm, like you are when you've been asleep long enough that the blanket and the body temperature have reached an agreement and everything is fine and there's no reason to be anywhere else.

I'm not anywhere else. I'm here. Wherever here is.

Something moves at the edge of it. A sound, maybe. A voice.

I don't chase it. It'll come back if it matters.

---

## Day Seven

"Can I ask you something," Wyaine says, over lunch, which by now has become a thing that just happens, no scheduling required, the two of us and whatever Bejors hasn't finished eating at her own desk.

"Go for it."

"You scared?"

I consider lying. Decide against it, mostly because he asked plainly and he deserves a plain answer back.

"Yeah," I say. "Most of the time. Not always about the same thing. Sometimes it's the mission, sometimes it's just, uh, whether I'm actually the right person for any of this."

"Why wouldn't you be."

"I didn't build any of this. I write books about things other people discovered. I'm not sure that's the same thing as being qualified to go find out what's actually out there."

Wyaine chews on that, and on his sandwich, for a while.

"Found the lip on a tray forty other people walked past for weeks," he says eventually. "Didn't need a doctorate for that. Just needed to actually look." He glances at me. "Maybe bein' qualified ain't the only thing that matters out there. Maybe payin' attention counts for somethin' too."

"That's oddly wise for a guy who calls calipers the pinchy thing."

"I contain multitudes," he says, entirely straight-faced, and goes back to his sandwich.

---

Warm.

Getting warmer, actually. I notice it now, like a tap left running somewhere. Not alarming. Just present. Slightly more there than it was a moment ago.

I'm comfortable. I think I'm comfortable.

Something pulls at me. A word, maybe. A number.

I don't have it yet.

---

## Day Eight

The days start blurring into each other the way work does when it's actually going well, small fixes stacking into bigger ones, Wyaine's unofficial lab access becoming so normalized that I catch myself forgetting he was ever just a driver. He's started keeping a running list, handwritten, tucked in his uniform pocket, of every small inconsistency he notices around the facility that isn't officially his problem to notice.

"What's that," I ask, catching him adding to it during a lull.

"Nothin'. Just stuff."

"Wyaine."

"Fine." He passes it over, a little sheepish. A dozen items, mostly mundane, a door that doesn't seal quite right, a warning light that flickers when it shouldn't, and then, near the bottom, in the same handwriting as everything else: *tray lip, day 4, fixed.*

"You're keeping a log," I say.

"I like keepin' track of things. Makes the big stuff feel smaller if you got a list of the little stuff you already handled."

I don't say anything for a second, because I'm busy deciding I like this person considerably more than I expected to like anyone I met by accident in an airport parking lot a week ago.

## Day Nine

Y'shari finds the three of us in the lab, late, the way she always seems to find us late, and stands in the doorway for a moment before saying anything.

"The launch window opens in four days," she says.

"We know," Bejors says, not looking up.

"I'm aware you know. I'm saying it out loud because I want to watch you all react to hearing it out loud, which tells me more than the calendar does." She looks at Wyaine specifically. "You've been remarkably difficult to keep out of a restricted facility for someone with no formal clearance."

"I'm very determined," Wyaine says.

"Apparently." She doesn't smile, exactly, but something in her posture loosens by a degree that I've learned to recognize as the closest she gets to it. "Keep at it. All of you. We're close."

She leaves. Wyaine watches the door for a second after she's gone.

"She's somethin', huh," he says.

"She's something," I agree, and don't elaborate, because I don't fully know how to yet.

---

Warm.

Very warm now. Too warm. There's something wrong with the warm.

I'm soaked.

The warm is wet. That's not right. That's not how warm is supposed to feel. Warm is dry and heavy and comfortable, not this, not this thing happening to the outside of me that my body is registering as wet and getting wetter, and the warm has gone somewhere past warm into something with a different name, something my skin is trying to report to me as urgent but the reporting mechanism isn't working right.

I try to move.

I can't move.

I try to open my eyes.

Nothing.

The wet gets worse. The wrong-warm gets worse. My body is telling me something about the outside of itself in terms I don't have access to right now but the gist of it is wrong wrong wrong wrong

---

## Day Ten

The satellite dish array is on the far side of the site from the lab, used for tracking and communication during launches. Y'shari wants to check the calibration before tonight's observation window. Bejors and I are along because neither of us actually went to the dorms and Y'shari didn't push it, which tells me she wanted the walk too.

We're still talking about the predator hypothesis. We can't help it. It's the kind of idea that follows you out of the room.

Y'shari says something. Bejors answers. I start to form a reply.

The flash comes first. White, total, gone before I register it as light at all, just an absence of every other visual input for a fraction of a second. Then the sound. Should be one sound, arrives as three or four stacked on top of each other, a crack and a boom and a pressure change that hits the chest before it reaches the ears.

Then the ground.

I'm already on the ground, paws in snow, ears doing something, cold suddenly the only fact in the world.

I look up.

The research building's topside is on fire.

The far wing is gone structurally. Nothing left of it but smoke, rising steady into the wind. The near wing is burning. The sky above it has gone orange and red.

I'm on my feet before I register standing. My legs move without waiting for approval. My body makes a decision about the building and executes it before I've formed an opinion, and I'm running before I know I'm running, four points of contact, paws punching snow, whatever runs underneath thought taking over once thought stops being fast enough.

The snow blurs past. The dishes blur past. I cross one road, then another. I can smell it now, the smell of a building that has stopped being a building, and my body keeps closing the distance because I don't have a better plan, and standing still in the cold watching a place full of people twenty seconds ago burn isn't one either.

It's hot. It shouldn't be this hot yet. I'm still a long way out, and the heat is already on me, on my face, my back, my chest, everywhere at once, too close, like it isn't coming from the building at all.

Forty yards. Maybe a little less.

Then nothing.

---

The nothing holds for a long time.

What comes back first is pain, which is at least familiar, and the sensation of having been too hot and not being too hot anymore. Relief. Real air. Something cycling. Cold. Real cold.

Then the arms.

I know the arms. I don't open my eyes yet but I know the arms by the sound and the grip, the same precision I woke up to on day one, and they're doing things to me that I'm choosing not to identify specifically. Medical things. Things that are probably necessary. I let them, because there aren't a lot of alternatives right now and at least someone is doing something.

I open my eyes.

White ceiling. Dorms and medbay. The arms working. The lights at the level they go when it's night on the ship's schedule, which means nothing specific but registers as quiet.

The bed to my left.

I look at it for a long time.

Jinzhou is in it.

Not asleep. In the berth. The one we woke up from, six years into a transit I don't remember agreeing to, the oval cubby that holds you and keeps you and apparently also lets you die in it without anyone knowing. The berth is slid out from the wall and the arms are working on him too, or were, they've settled into a monitoring configuration now. Just present. Just watching.

There's a sealed wound on his left arm that I can see from here. Not from the EVA suit. Wrong location for a suit breach.

Something that happened getting me back inside. Something that happened while I was unconscious and cooking alive on the hull of the ship and couldn't do anything about it or know anything about it or stop him from going out after me.

I look at the ceiling.

Then I look at him again.

Then I try to get up, which I shouldn't do, and find out two things at once. The first is that the arms have an opinion about it. The second is that nothing weighs anything. The strap across my chest is the only thing that agrees with me about where the berth is, and the ceiling is getting politely farther away.

The arms close in, firm and unhurried.

"Rest, Dr. Vasek," ARACCSIM says, from somewhere farther off than it should be.

Across the gap, the readout on his frame is doing the things readouts are supposed to do.

He's in there. He's going to be fine.

I keep my eyes on the readout for as long as I can. It isn't very long.

---

## Chapter Twelve, continued: JINZHOU T. KENZO

Altitude was on panel four.

Secondary display. Three hundred and ten meters of drift. Didn't see it. I was watching the winch, because Ethyn asked me to watch the winch, because the winch was the problem.

All true. None of it useful.

Winch load off the main screen. Altitude on. Worse than three hundred and ten now.

External camera. The tank covers are coming off. One, then the next, then the next. Not falling. Lifting off the housings and going. Tank 2-E is bare.

Ethyn turns toward the airlock.

The locker goes.

Two seconds. Locker leaves the rail. Ethyn is still turning. Locker comes down on him. He folds. Doesn't unfold. The tether takes him and he swings out and hangs at the end of it. Aft starboard. Hot side. Facing down at Bejors and the cloud we came here for.

"Ethyn."

Comm is open. A hiss. His breathing, slow. Sleeping breath.

"Ethyn. *Ethyn.* Answer me. Answer me right now, you, Ethyn, *answer*-"

My voice goes up. Then it breaks in the middle, like a cable taking a load it isn't rated for, and what comes out the other side isn't a word.

"Damn it," I say. Empty cockpit. "Damn it. Damn it, Ethyn."

Never said that word out loud before. I'll note it later. Not now.

Vitals still on my screen. Heart rate steady. I look at it for one second. Exactly one. That second is the only reason I don't do something stupid in the one after.

The stupid thing is full climb. All motors, one pulse, one point seven out of nothing. Tether is rated for two. The locker mount it's caught under isn't rated for anything anymore. Ethyn on the end of that, out cold, stops being a problem I can solve.

So. Not all at once.

Throttle is two things. The trigger fires one pulse. Center ring first, then the wave runs out ring by ring to the edge of the plate. Hold the trigger and the pulses keep coming. The lever says how many motors are in the wave. That's all it does. Simple system. Works.

I don't touch either one. I write over them.

LEVER: RAMP. START 120. +100 EVERY 3RD PULSE.
CAP 900. FINAL STEP ON CREW AUTH.
TRIGGER: HOLD.
OMIT: ARACCSIM SELECT. WAVE EVEN. EXCLUDE 6.

Eleven seconds. Enter.

"Ramp accepted," ARACCSIM says. "Selecting omitted motors. Motor six excluded."

Pulse. Center ring. The wave goes out under my feet.

Pulse.

Pulse. A hundred more.

Weight. Not the centrifuge's 1G. Engines. Not all at once, either. It comes in steps. Every third pulse the ship leans on me a little harder. Down is aft now, and it keeps getting more down.

Ethyn is down.

On the camera his tether straightens. Slow. Takes up the slack. Holds. Good.

Winch finishes on its own. Probe in the bay in ninety seconds. ARACCSIM can have that one.

I'm on the ladder.

---

Suit-up. In order.

Suit on in four minutes twenty. Checklist minimum is three forty. Forty seconds slow. Fine. The floor gets heavier every half minute. Every piece weighs more than it did in training, and more than it did a minute ago.

Tether rated for two. Second line clipped to the primary before I cycle.

Ethyn on the cameras. Not moving. Suit intact. No breach indicators. Unconscious, hanging, heating up. Thermal rating on his telemetry: fourteen minutes at current external temperature. After that, his core temperature is a medical emergency.

Fourteen.

My suit is going out into the same heat his is. I don't run that number. Running it doesn't change it.

Airlock opens.

---

Two weeks of this hull on screens. On a screen it's geometry. Distances. Angles.

Out here it's a slope.

The Vanguard is tipping. Nose down. We're still pointed retrograde, motors still set to brake, and there aren't enough of them in the wave yet to hold us level. So the ship sags toward the planet, front first, slow. Down is aft, and aft is downhill.

Out of the airlock. Up onto the hull. The rail runs from here all the way to the engines, straight down the slope. Ethyn is at the bottom of it.

I don't climb down. I let the weight do it.

Feet on the rails. Paws on the rails. Nothing on the rungs. I slide.

The orange goes past. The layer is right on top of us, pale, close enough to touch. Cells hit me on the way down. The motors are misfiring and whatever goes wide lands on the hull. Small. Hard. Often. I could count them. I don't.

Debris coming down the slope with me. Bracket. Strut. A cover panel, door-sized. I turn my shoulder. It takes the outer layer of my suit and the paint off the rail and keeps going. So do I.

"Tank 2-E, breach," ARACCSIM says. "Auto-seal engaged."

Keep going.

"Seal complete. Ingress interval, four point one seconds. Ambient cloud density at breach, high."

I know what's in the cloud. It's why we came. Now it's in a fuel tank. Four point one seconds is long enough.

Nothing I can do about a tank from out here. It goes on the list. The list is long.

Junction bracket. I clamp. Paws, then feet. The rail is hot through the gloves. I stop.

Ethyn is where the tether says he is.

Hanging. Suit intact. Inside of his visor fogged a little. Fog means breathing. Breathing matters more than the tether.

His primary is caught under what's left of the locker mount. No time to work it free. I unclip it from the mount and snap it to my harness. Second line to his suit anchor. Now I'm the mount.

Start up.

He's heavy.

Pulse. Pulse. Pulse. A hundred more. Every third one he gets heavier, and so do I, and so does the slope. Feels like two point five. Probably isn't. Feels like it. Not a complaint. A number I'm guessing at.

One paw. Reel. One paw. Reel. I'm not climbing him. I'm dragging him. Up a hill made of a ship, with the cloud on my visor and the rail ringing every time something hits it.

Thermal clock on his telemetry. Thirteen. Twelve.

My arms say stop. Something under my arms says no. Adrenaline. I note it's there. It pulls. I pull.

If my existence was trivial before, it means everything now.

Don't know when I started thinking that. Somewhere on the rail. Doesn't matter when. It's true. Keep climbing.

---

Airlock hatch above me. Then level. Then I'm pushing Ethyn through it headfirst. Not how the manual does it. The manual doesn't cover this.

He goes in. All of him. I let go of the line.

Something hits my left arm.

Hard. The suit makes a short noise in my ear. Then it stops making it.

I look down.

A length of rod from the locker mount. Rebar. Through my forearm. In one side, out the other. The suit has already sealed around both holes, because the suit is better at its job than I am at mine today.

I look at it for one second. Exactly one.

Then I take hold of the end and pull. It comes. I let the weight have it. It slides away down the slope toward the engines and is gone.

I don't feel it yet. I'll feel it later. I go in after him.

"ARACCSIM. Crew inside. Final step."

"Final step authorized."

Outer door closes. The last hundred join the wave. Center ring, out to the edge, every motor on the plate but six. The weight comes down on both of us like a hand on a table. Through the little window the hull is coming apart past the glass. Panel. Strut. Bracket. All of it going aft. The nose comes up. The orange thins. Goes. Dark at the edges of the glass. Something loud hits the hull and is gone.

Last pulse fires clean.

Engines cut. Weight goes. Down stops being a direction. Ethyn drifts off the floor against the inner door, and I catch him by the harness.

"Orbit achieved."

Four seconds. I give myself four seconds.

"Orbit altitude is below the Stephfren line," it continues. "Shield envelope absent. Decay projected within six days. Contingency return is available. It does not require the transfer window. It adds seven months to the transit. It will initiate in sixty minutes unless crew authority is registered."

Ethyn's vitals on my suit display. Heart rate steady. Breathing even. Whatever the locker did, it's doing it quietly.

Left arm. Last item.

Hole through the forearm. Entry. Exit. The suit sealed itself around both. I didn't feel it while the rod was in. I can feel it now. The arm has been reporting the heat for twenty minutes. I wasn't reading the reports.

"Mr. Kenzo," ARACCSIM says. "Suit thermal layer, left forearm, compromised since impact. Exposure exceeded rating by six minutes. Recommend immediate treatment."

"I know."

Then the adrenaline goes. All at once. The pain comes in order. Left arm first. Then the rest.

Transfer window: two hundred and eighty-two days. I know the number. I put it where I put things.

"ARACCSIM. Hold the return."

"Holding requires continuous crew authority."

"Keep him stable."

"Dr. Vasek is stable."

"Good."

I try to note that.

The note doesn't finish.

*END OF CHAPTER TWELVE*
