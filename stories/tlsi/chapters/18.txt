
I step through first.

Not because I'm brave about it. Jinzhou made it clear without a word that he'll be the one covering the doorway if this goes sideways, and I've decided the least I can do is be the one who walks into the room that might go sideways.

The cockpit module is small. Smaller than ours. Everything in here has the compressed, over-organized look of a space three people have lived in for a lot longer than three people are supposed to live in a room this size. Gear is lashed to every surface. A blanket is folded with military precision and strapped to what used to be a seat and is now, apparently, also a bed.

And there are three of them.

All three are hooked onto rails and loops against the far wall, in a loose cluster. Not a formation. It's what you look like after months of not knowing if the next thing through the door is help or the end.

The one who was talking to us, I can tell before he opens his mouth again. He's the one in front, the one who put himself between us and the other two without seeming to think about it, drifting half a meter side to side with one foot hooked on a rail, like holding still isn't an option for him right now.

"Thats far enough," he says. "Yo seriously- just chill for a sec, alright- paws where i can see em"

"Hi," I say, paws still up. "Far enough. We can do far enough."

"Who are you," he says. "For real this time, not the yellin version"

"Ethyn Vasek. Secondary Scientist, Vanguard MK1. This is Jinzhou Kenzo, Software Engineer, same ship." I keep my voice level, or as level as I can manage, which right now is not especially level. "We picked up your transponder burst on approach. We didn't, uh, know MK2 existed until about eight hours ago."

That gets a reaction from the other two, a look passed between them that I can't fully read. Something between confirmation and disappointment, like a theory just got proven right that they'd been hoping would turn out wrong.

"You didn't know we existed," the blunt one repeats.

"No."

"Nobody told you a second ship was comin? for real? seriously?"

"No."

He laughs. It isn't a happy sound. Short and sharp. He suspected it, and he hates being right.

"Course they fuckin didnt," he says, mostly to the other two, not really to us. "Course! wow! cool! great! love that for us! am i wrong- tell me im wrong"

Nobody tells him he's wrong.

---

The one on the left drifts forward slightly, not aggressive, just present.

"Marsh Okonjo," she says. "Pilot."

Then, from further back, slower, unhurried, someone pushes off the wall like leaving it is a decision he's thought all the way through first.

"Petryn Draven," he says. Not loud. Not needing to be. "Engineer."

There's a pause after his name, like he's giving the room a second to receive it before he keeps going. It's a very different thing from how the blunt one talks, where every sentence seems to be racing the next one out of his mouth.

"Man," Petryn says, looking at us properly now, at the suits, at the ship somewhere out past the hull that neither of them can see but both know is there. "You two came a long way to find us lookin' like this. Sorry about the mess. We weren't really expectin' company."

"Petryn." The blunt one, sharp, like Petryn said something wrong by being calm about it. "We dont know these guys"

"I know we don't know 'em, Maslin," he says, easy, unbothered, the words coming out slow and rounded at the edges like he's got all the time left in the universe to say them. "That's kinda the whole thing about meetin' people. You don't know 'em yet."

Maslin doesn't have a response to that right away, which I get the sense doesn't happen to him often.

"Maslin Corwin," he says eventually, to us, still watching like he's deciding whether what I've told him changes how this conversation is supposed to go. "Quartermaster, or was, not sure the title means shit anymore given theres nothin left to quarter out"

"There's some," Petryn says. Not correcting him, exactly. Just adding to it, gently. "Not much. Some."

"Sure man whatever-" Maslin says, "-this is some reaalll robust supply chain we got goin, world fuckin class."

I look between the three of them. Marsh is still in a careful way, like she's been rationing energy along with everything else. Petryn is still in a loose way. He decided a while back that panic wasn't helping and just stopped doing it. I'd like to know how. I'd like a pamphlet.

Maslin is the one still closest to us. Still the one most ready to move if he needs to.

"How long," I ask, "have you three been in here?"

"Define in here," Maslin says. "This specific room, or like, in general, trapped on a ship goin nowhere fast"

"Either. Both."

He looks at Marsh. Marsh looks back, and something passes between them, the shorthand of people who've had this exact conversation enough times that they no longer need most of the words.

"Cockpit specifically," Petryn says, when neither of the other two seems inclined to answer first, unhurried, like he's telling a story rather than reporting a fact. "About four months, man. We were livin' in the rest of the ship before that. Storage room, mostly, some of the crew section. Sealed ourselves in here once the last of the hull breaches got bad enough we couldn't trust the pressure out there no more."

"And before that," I ask. "The explosion. What happened?"

Maslin's jaw does something tight.

"Fuel reaction," he says, fast now, the words tumbling. "Somethin in the transfer system went sideways- blew two tanks, cooked a third, hull breach right through the crew section." He gestures vaguely at the door behind us, at the dark hallway beyond it. "We got lucky- relatively- all three of us made it out. Ship didnt."

"Relatively," I repeat.

"You want me to say unlucky instead?! fine! unlucky! we got real reaalll unlucky and then a little bit of lucky that the fuckedup part didnt just straight up kill us and- uhm- weve been runnin that shit math ever since tryin to find the actual thing between those two and, uh, spoiler alert buddy, it is not a fun awnser"

Petryn puts a paw on his shoulder. Just that. Doesn't say anything about it, doesn't make it a whole thing, just a paw, steady, there.

"He does that," he says to me, easy, like he's letting me in on something rather than explaining an inconvenience. "Talks it all the way out till there's nothin' left in the tank. It's how he gets through it. I don't mind it none."

"I dont need you narratin me, Petryn," Maslin says, but there's no real heat in it, and he doesn't shrug the paw off either.

"I know you don't need it," he says. "I'm doin' it anyway. That's kinda my whole thing."

I don't have a response to any of that that doesn't feel inadequate, so I don't try to give one.

---

"You said you didn't know about us," Marsh says, into the quiet that follows, her first full sentence since her name. "But you know the mission. You know why we were coming."

"Resupply," I say. "ARACCSIM, our ship's system, it pulled that much off your transponder data. You were meant to reach us. Support our mission. Extended duration capability."

"Extended duration," Maslin says, and laughs again, that same short unhappy sound. "Thats a real cute way of puttin it. You know what we got told when we launched- nine people, a year or two of supplies, three of us, a decade if we went into extended sleep for most of it. Were the safe gig, allegedly! the easy one- well uh, i guess, past tense, none of that shits true anymore now is it?" He gestures at the room around us, at the blanket folded into a bed, the lashed-down gear, the four months of living in a sealed box that used to be a cockpit. "This was, uhm- this was thee safe assignment. This right here."

"I'm sorry," I say, because it's the only thing I have, and it feels small next to everything he just said, and I say it anyway because small is still something.

Maslin looks at me for a long moment.

"Yeah," he says, finally, some of the edge going out of his voice, not gone, just set down for a second. "Yeah. Me too man. Me too."

Petryn tilts his head slightly, looking at the two of us with something that isn't quite a smile but is heading in that direction.

"Crazy, though," he says, slow, thoughtful, like he's turning the thought over before letting it out. "Two ships, launched months apart, endin' up in the same little pocket of the whole universe at the same time. That's, like. That's a lot of somethin' had to line up for that to happen, you know? I don't know if I believe in luck exactly. But I believe in that."

Nobody has an answer for that either, and this time the quiet that follows doesn't feel bad. It feels like a room full of people letting something settle.

"How many of you made it," Marsh finally asks, direct, no padding around it. "You said two. Out of."

"Six," I say. "Out of six."

Nobody says anything to that for a while. It's a silence that doesn't need filling, five of us hooked onto rails in a cramped, dying ship's cockpit, doing the same quiet arithmetic in our heads about how much worse this could have been and how much it already is regardless.

"Well," Maslin says eventually, and there's something almost like an attempt at levity in it, worn thin but trying. "Guess between the five of us we make one halfway decent crew, frankensteined together outta spare parts, not pretty but hey, its somethin"

Jinzhou, from the doorway, says nothing, which is exactly what I expect from him.

Then, quiet, almost to himself, he adds, "Six, if you count ARACCSIM."

Maslin looks at him like he wasn't expecting the flat one to make a joke, and honestly, neither was I.

"Ha! okay, him i like already," Maslin says, jerking his chin at Jinzhou. "You, im still workin on, youre the one who screamed at me through a door about a suit for like a full minute straight"

"I have a lot of feelings and very little control over the volume of them," I say. "It's a whole thing."

"No shit," Maslin says.

Petryn laughs, low and easy, with a little exhale on the end, like a piece of the last four months just got out.

"I like these two," he says, to nobody in particular, to the room, to whatever's left of the crew he's got. "This is good, man. This is real good."

And for a second, just a second, something in the room loosens that wasn't loose a minute ago.

---

I'm the one who brings up the plants. I don't plan to. It just comes out, the way the important questions always seem to come out of me sideways.

"The plants," I say. "In the storage room. Somebody's been watering them."

Marsh's ears move. It's a very small motion, and she corrects it almost as soon as it happens, but I see it.

"Those are mine," she says.

"They're doing well. Considering."

"They are doing adequately." She says it the way she says everything, careful, every word weighed before it's spent. "They were cargo. Seed stock, for a garden module on your ship that I now understand you do not have. When the tanks went, the cargo hold went with them. Those six pots were in the storage room for a germination check. They are what is left."

"So you kept them alive."

"Somebody had to." A pause. "It gave the day a shape. Water at the start of the cycle, check the wicks, adjust the lamp. It is very hard to give up on a day that has a shape."

I think about the berth I woke up in, and the whiteboard, and every list I've made since. I don't say anything. I don't have to. She sees me not saying it, and I think she understands it better than if I had.

"And the marks," I say. "By the door. On the outside."

Maslin, who has been doing his best impression of a man not listening, makes a noise.

"thats me," he says. "marsh cant suit up and seal the hatch behind her at the same time, so somebody had to go out with her, so i went out with her, every time. and every time i went past the door i did a line"

"A hundred and twenty-two."

He blinks at me. "you counted em"

"I count things. It's a condition."

"huh." He looks at me a little differently. Not much. A notch. "i was gonna stop at a hundred and fifty. i figured if nobody came by a hundred and fifty i was gonna, uh. i dunno. i didnt get that far in the plan"

"You don't have to finish the plan," Petryn says, quiet.

"i know i dont have to finish it, im sayin i didnt"

"I know, man. I'm sayin you don't have to."

Some of the deep ones, the gouges that went down to bare metal, I think I understand now. I don't ask which days those were. Some numbers are a person's own business.

"Then the count stops at a hundred and twenty-two," I say. "That's a good number. I like it. It's got a two at the end, which means it's even, which means it's done."

"thats not how numbers work"

"It's how they work today."

Maslin looks at me for a long second, and then he laughs, actually laughs, a short surprised bark that seems to catch him off guard as much as anyone.

"youre so weird," he says.

"I've been told."

---

"The hallway holds pressure now," Jinzhou says from the doorway, because somebody has to say the next practical thing and it's never going to be me while I'm busy having feelings about tally marks. "You can cross without suits."

Maslin stares at him. "for real"

"We filled it."

"The air you put in that hallway," Marsh says. "You bled your own ship to open our door."

"Twice."

"That is not nothing."

"It's what it cost," I say. "It was the cheapest thing on the list."

Petryn looks at me with that slow almost-smile again, the one that seems to take its time arriving.

"See, I told you," he says to Maslin. "Good people."

"you said that about the guy who sold us the water filters"

"And I was wrong about him, man. I'm allowed to be wrong once."

*END OF CHAPTER EIGHTEEN*
